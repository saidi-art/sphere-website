Sphere by KubeAI, a floating capture tool for Android

Capture here, switch, drop there.

A small sphere floats over your other apps. Double-tap it for an instant screenshot; a single idle tap does nothing, so captures are never accidental. Drag it onto its bottom target and it makes a stitched long screenshot, hands-free. Long-press opens its tools, including REC for long, text-dense threads.

REC turns what it records into searchable text on your phone; other captures come back as images (Image / Open / Share). Captures and their text are processed on your phone and stay there unless you choose to share. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking.

An instant screenshot, exactly where you drop it.

Double-tap the idle sphere and it takes the screenshot. A single idle tap deliberately does nothing, so a capture never happens by accident. The result appears in a popup with its own actions, staying for about five seconds; a tap outside dismisses it and still reaches the app underneath.

From there, hand the capture into another app: capture here, switch, drop there.

A whole thread, tap and leave.

Long-press the sphere and choose Rescue: it scrolls back toward the top of the chat, then captures its way back down while you get on with something else. A Cancel/Done bar stays available throughout, and your phone is kept awake during the run.

Sphere hands over exactly what it captured: no rewriting, no summaries. Direct text, which reads the screen through Android's accessibility API instead of the pixels, is optional, experimental, off by default and separately consented.

REC: record a long thread, keep the words.

Long-press the sphere and choose REC. Sphere explains what is about to happen in its own design, then Android asks for screen-recording consent. Recording runs with the sphere, a speed bar and the Cancel/Done bar on screen, and auto-scroll is already running.

REC is for big captures: long threads and lots of text. The speed bar sets the pace from 0 to 10. The app’s own first-use tip recommends speed 10 for faster results on long scroll threads, and notes that slower scrolling means longer processing.

At speed 0, you scroll yourself while recording continues. The oval thumb holds pause and play: one deliberate tap pauses or resumes scrolling and recording together, and dragging the thumb changes speed. The speed bar can be repositioned like the sphere, with a generous drag area.

Finish with a single tap (Done) or the Cancel/Done bar. Processing follows as its own phase, with a short sound cue near the end. The result arrives in a notification offering Text / Open / Share.

Every tap is deliberate.

  • Idle: double-tap captures; a single tap does nothing.
  • Long-press: the tools: Rescue, REC, Auto-scroll and Tools.
  • During Rescue, Auto capture or REC: one tap finishes (Done), two taps cancel.
  • The Cancel/Done bar does both visibly, and is the accessible route (TalkBack).
  • Taps are deliberately firm: a light haptic tick when the first tap registers, and distinct Done and Cancel feedback, so you never lose work to a misread tap.
  • Sounds: an auto-scroll sound and an end-of-processing cue, each switchable in Settings.

Your captures, your folder.

Results arrive as notifications with actions: REC captures offer Text / Open / Share; other captures offer Image / Open / Share. The notification shade is always the fallback route back to a capture.

Captures save as ordinary files in Download/Sphere, one subfolder per account, with titles drawn from what is in them rather than date stamps. Files already on your phone are never moved or deleted by the app.

Sphere does not rewrite or summarise what it captures. Handing a capture to another app is always your own deliberate act.

Signed in, and yours.

Sign-in is required before capturing: email and password, or Google. Sign-in runs through Firebase Authentication, a Google service. After the first sign-in, one small card asks what to call you, once, with your Google name prefilled where available and editable later in Settings.

That name supplies the sphere’s initial, drawn as a serif letterform, brass on Night desk. Your captures save under your account’s own subfolder in Download/Sphere.

Signing out or deleting your account stops the sphere and new captures; capture files already on your phone stay. Deletion lives in Settings and is also honoured by email.

A new colour every day

Day theme is generated, not chosen from a set. The sphere's colour comes from the day of the year: its hue is the day number times the golden angle (137.50776 degrees), drawn in OKLCH, so each of the 365 days of the year has its own colour. No two days look the same, and the same date looks the same every year.

Night desk is a fixed material set: emerald cased glass and polished brass, like a banker's lamp on a dark desk. It never changes by day. The dial above shows today's palette at the shell's real offsets: Rescue 120 degrees away, Auto-scroll 240, the record button 300, Tools the complement at 180, softened.

Where Sphere is heading

One calm point in a crowd of agents: no more copy, paste and re-explain. Every agent gets the same context from one place. This is a future direction, not what Sphere does today.
